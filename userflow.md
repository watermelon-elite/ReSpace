# User flow, screen by screen

0. Welcome (1 tap): "What space are we working on?" → My home / My café or business. Business mode adds a seating-capacity target and walkway checks (keep aisles at least 36"/91 cm wide), and allows quantities such as "×8 chairs".

1. Scan (60–120 s): use RoomPlan's built-in scanning screen as is. It already shows coaching like "move closer to the wall" and draws the outline live. While scanning, quietly grab 3–4 camera frames. Gemini uses them to read the room's style, colors and floor material.

2. "Here's your room": a top-down 3D view of the room. Detected items have labels ("Sofa · 2.1 m", "Table", "2 Chairs"), and you can tap one to mark it Keep. This shows off the scan and gives the user a sense of ownership right away.

3. Intent sheet:

- Three large cards: ✨ Add a few things · 📍 Find something for a spot · 🔄 Full redesign
- Style chips: Cozy · Minimal · Japandi · Industrial · Boho · Mid-century · "Match what I have"
- Budget slider (Business mode adds "Seats: 24")
- Text box: "Or just tell us…" (e.g. "warm reading nook by the window, plants, under $400")
- "Find something for a spot" goes to the room view instead: tap a spot on the floor or wall, then answer "What goes here?"
4. Generating (10–20 s): show progress that tells a story, which hides the wait: "Measuring your 3.4 m window wall… Finding rugs that fit under your sofa…"

5. Design screen (the main screen):

- 3D room with the new items placed. Items that were moved slide to their new spots, and removed items appear as faded ghosts.
- Before/After toggle.
Bottom sheet listing the items with photo, price and store, plus the total ("$612 · 7 items").
- Tap an item: Swap (3 alternatives) · Move (drag on the floor, rotate with two fingers) · Remove · Buy.
- Chat bar at the bottom: "cheaper rug", "make it feel warmer", "move the sofa to the window wall". Each message triggers a small edit, not a full redo.
- Day / Evening toggle (see the lighting section).
6. Walk mode: the "Street View" idea. A joystick on the left moves you, dragging on the right looks around, and the camera sits at eye height (1.5 m). Also let people tap the floor to jump there, which is easier for non-gamers. Make the top-down view the default and walk mode a toggle. Most people find top-down easier to use.

7. Save / share / shopping list: a link list sorted by store, and a screenshot of the design to share.
