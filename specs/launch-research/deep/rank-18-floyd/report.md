## Strongest ReSpace applications — editorial inference

1. **Join real room, room model and real object.** Use a repeated viewpoint or object to connect the physical room to the captured geometry, then to the proposed placement. Avoid suggesting the app magically changes the physical room.
2. **Let a constraint reveal personality.** A meaningful object that stays, a work area that needs room or a route that needs to remain clear gives spatial personality something concrete to mean. Show the constraint and its consequence in the proposal.
3. **Use one physical detail as evidence.** A chair position, clearance or real product selected for a reason can be more persuasive than a rapid list of AI capabilities. Current SPEC.md describes real products and purchase links; only show the flow and data confirmed in the build being filmed.
4. **Give the result a human pause.** After a short, clear app sequence, return to the person and the room. A look, movement or everyday action can communicate the intended benefit without a claim about guaranteed wellbeing.

Do not copy Detroit, simulated film grain or workshop imagery merely to borrow credibility. Use the team's actual process and the user's actual room. The specification describes intended behavior; it is not evidence that every screen or interaction is ready to film.
