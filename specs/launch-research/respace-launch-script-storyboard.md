# ReSpace launch film script and storyboard

A barren dorm could belong to anyone. Its new owner needs somewhere to study, make room for friends and work out what feels like him. ReSpace turns those intentions into specific questions, a taste profile and a proposed room with real products. The imagined future returns to the same assignment and card game established in the opening.

The emotional story, app and closing occupy **156 seconds, or 87% of the film**. Technical, business and future directions retain three eight-second reservations. The [critical review](respace-script-revision-review.md) explains the changes against the strongest film references.

## Format and performance

- **Master:** 03:00, 16:9 landscape, 43 timed shots.
- **Voiceover:** 224 core words, approximately 75 words per minute averaged across the film. The fuller product explanation raises the earlier 172-word draft by 52 words. Read conversationally, around 130–155 words per minute while speaking, with phrases flowing across cuts. The remaining space belongs to actions, readable interfaces, music and reactions. The three supporting slots have no finished narration yet.
- **Product timing:** ReSpace enters at 00:24; the first complete proposal appears at 00:58. Generated questions and the real-product card receive longer holds than the opening object cuts.
- **Cast and location:** one student, two friends, one actual dorm. The same friends appear in an early photograph and the imagined card game.
- **Tone:** observant, warm and lightly funny. His life supplies the reason for every product action.
- **Production:** the existing bed, desk and chair remain in place. Film real people on real contact surfaces, then composite the imagined surroundings around them. No furniture purchase is required.
- **Continuity:** repeat the doorway, window, notebook, card deck and photograph. Keep the actual room proposal visible beside the photograph in the final scene.
- **CTA:** ReSpace. Make room for you. Join the waitlist.

## Directions for the reserved sections

These are brainstorming directions inside fixed time reservations. Their detailed wording and finished graphics can be developed after the emotional story and app sequence work.

### Technical idea

**Primary direction: make the same room briefly explain itself.** The photograph and room tile become a measured outline; proposed objects appear in relation to the bed and desk; a visible fit check resolves. Three actions are enough: Measure, Plan, Check.

If small technical labels help the intended audience, associate RoomPlan and LiDAR plus Perceptron with measurement/perception, Gemini with planning, and the placement solver with geometry checks. The useful distinction is that AI chooses relationships while deterministic code computes and checks placement. Keep the recognizable dorm underneath the graphic.

An alternative emphasis is one lamp: how the system recognizes the available space, chooses a real product with dimensions, and checks its proposed placement. This is more tangible than a provider-logo wall. **Research-informed layout** can be a restrained supporting caption where the actual planning rules use that research; show the practical concern, such as study space or access, rather than a claim of a psychologically optimal room.

### Business idea

**Primary direction: follow the lamp's purchase link.** Its card connects to a retailer, and a modest branch represents affiliate revenue helping support a generous free experience. The viewer already knows why the product is relevant, so the model can be understood without a financial slide.

Suggested graphic language: Generous free tier → Relevant product links → Affiliate revenue. Treat this as the intended business model, not a report of earned revenue or signed merchant agreements. Do not use fictional sales, commission rates, margins or conversion figures.

The commercial hypothesis is that personalized, dimension-checked recommendations can connect planning with purchase intent. Viability still depends on qualifying purchase conversion, merchant terms, commissions, and the cost of serving free users. Those belong in later business work; an eight-second film passage should convey the mechanism. Any eventual product-link disclosure should be clear where the link is offered.

A secondary visual possibility is to keep the user's plan accessible while the product link does the commercial work. Avoid implying that purchasing furniture is required to explore a design or that every operation is unlimited.

### Future directions idea

**Primary direction: the same room outline becomes part of a larger place.** First show a multi-room home; then repeat the graphic grammar in a small café. Mark the passage Future direction and give each setting one visual beat.

The connection is personal space → whole home → a place people gather. These are the agreed roadmap themes. AR, additional monetization paths and other possible features do not need to compete for this eight-second slot. No delivery date is proposed.

### Traction

No traction segment is included. If actual Reddit post evidence becomes available, it can replace part of a supporting slot or be added to a separate pitch version. A post-view count should retain its source and observation date.

## Filming the imagined future without buying furniture

Use **two locked camera positions**: a doorway wide and a desk/bed medium. Take clean empty-room plates from each. Mark tripod feet, lens, camera height and actor positions. Keep focus, exposure and white balance manual between the plates and performances.

Protect the current bed and desk in the app's design. The student studies at the real desk and chair. The two friends sit on the real bed; the student turns the existing chair toward them. Cards are dealt onto the existing bed. Every person sits, leans and places objects on a real surface.

Add the proposed environment around those performances: an unoccupied lamp, storage, art or other choices actually present in the design. Preserve real floor and contact shadows under the actors; keep a digital rug away from their feet or omit it. Match the render's perspective and light to the filmed plate. Keep the window, floor boundaries and architectural proportions stable. A small digital push is added only after the composite is finished; a handheld walk around people and invented furniture would require much more tracking and reconstruction.

Film shots 27–30 as one continuous locked-wide performance, with enough resolution to crop the first beat. Use the second fixed camera only if the crop lacks detail. Mark a clear standing area for the departing friend and keep added objects out of that path. This preserves the reactions and reduces the sequence to a small number of reusable composites.

The **actual photorealistic proposal** is first shown inside the app. The next image is an explicitly identified editorial imagined future, with real actors composited into it. When returning to the phone, show the room proposal without the actors. This avoids implying that ReSpace generates a movie of the user's friends.

For the evening, use existing or borrowed lighting and film a matching plate. A color grade can unify the shots but cannot convincingly invent a new light direction on a face. Record a complete card-game exchange with the actual friends, including the attempted departure and extra dealt hand, rather than asking everyone to pose as if laughing. There is no need for intelligible improvised dialogue.

Use one loose printed photograph throughout, with no frame required. It starts tucked in his notebook and is finally displayed against it, with the saved room proposal visible beside it. The card game supplies the joke in the imagined future. The photograph is a film motif; ReSpace does not identify its people or infer their personalities from the image.

## Pacing and sound direction

**First seven seconds:** the opening wide establishes geography; three one-second object details establish sameness. Each cut adds to a short rhythm. Move on as soon as the idea is understood.

**Seven to twenty-four seconds:** the notebook, photograph and cards supply a specific life for the room to accommodate. The pencil and card sounds return in the imagined future. End with a practical question and his decision to open ReSpace.

**The product turn:** retain a hand or part of the actual room around the app. Scanning and budget setting are familiar actions and can move quickly. Slow down for the generated question, its answer and the visible consequence. A practical question should develop his goal rather than ask him to repeat it.

**The first result at 00:58:** the chosen material and study lamp become visible in a recognizable room. Keep the full proposal readable. The subsequent product card has seven seconds to establish size, price and the merchant link. Avoid an extended beauty orbit that delays this evidence.

**The imagined evening:** move from pencil and page sounds into cards and a backpack strap. Give the friend time to notice the extra dealt hand and decide to stay. The narration's repeated “one more” begins with study and ends as a small joke. Leave the final wide without voiceover.

**Refinement and community:** the smaller lamp solves a practical issue revealed by seeing the first proposal. Hold request and result in consistent geography. Community then expands the idea to other people's spaces; sharing gives someone else an example to start from.

**Supporting passage and close:** use one short graphic vocabulary for the three reserved ideas. Return to the original room tone at 02:42. The saved proposal and newly displayed photograph share the final composition, showing both a plan and the student's first small action.

Use an instrumental track with a restrained opening, a fuller middle and a warm resolution. Keep lyrics out of the narration's way. Subtitle the spoken script; additional text should identify a concept or result rather than repeat every sentence as a second headline.

## Product capture and finishing notes

The creative feature brief includes photorealistic exploration, community posts, generated questions and natural-language taste learning, computer vision, research-informed layout and a configurable budget, as confirmed in the interview. It is a production brief, not an implementation change to SPEC.md. Capture each product feature from the version being promoted; the imagined-life composites remain separately identified film effects.

Keep the recorded room, chosen budget, products, prices and displayed result internally consistent. Choose the actual lamp replacement before filming so shots 22, 32 and 33 form a clear input-and-result chain. Preserve a visible editorial transition across processing; the cut is not a speed benchmark. Use generated questions that clarify missing details rather than repeat known answers. The narration says questions are created around the room and goals; it does not claim every answer generates a new branch. Show the actual questions and resulting choice, without inventing a separate profile screen. “Taste profile” uses the user-confirmed creative feature brief and does not imply personality inference or learning across unrelated rooms.

The restrained research caption requires a traceable connection from the cited guidance to the product's planning or evaluation. The [layout research summary](../layout-awareness-side-chat-summary.md) supports the distinction between personal preferences, activity needs and general design guidance; it does not establish a universally optimal layout or a psychological outcome from this app.

Community content should be usable with permission or explicitly staged as demo content. Show proposed designs as designs, with no fabricated engagement figures. The post-preparation shot can be recorded without publishing.

Before export, supply the working waitlist destination, final brand assets, a complete recorded design/edit session and the exact photorealistic room views used for compositing. The end card already has its final CTA wording; an unknown URL has not been invented.
