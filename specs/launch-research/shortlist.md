# ReSpace launch-film research: 120 candidates → 20 priority references

**Full-film follow-up:** all twenty selected films now have [complete audiovisual analyses and an updated comparison](deep/index.md), plus an [original ReSpace creative direction](deep/respace-direction.md). The evidence labels and open questions below preserve the earlier selection stage; use the deep reports for current footage findings and source-version corrections.

**Recommendation: build the creative direction around Pinterest’s spatial personality, reMarkable’s worldview-to-product argument, and Wispr Flow’s human-problem-to-software transition.** Use IKEA Kreativ and IKEA Place to keep the product proof understandable. This is a selection-stage research report; the twenty below are the next deep-viewing queue, not twenty completed shot-by-shot teardowns.

Research captured 17 October 2026, America/Los_Angeles; some API responses are dated 18 October UTC. The brief is a professional, fast-paced launch for ReSpace: a room-scanning and redesign iPhone app whose story begins with people and their environment. Creative applications below are **editorial inferences**, not proven explanations of a video’s reach.

## Scope and evidence

- **120 distinct candidates screened:** 52 spatial/home references, 38 creative startup/product references, 7 additional launch films, 8 additional YouTube discoveries, and 15 provisional X launch leads. The first 105 already exceed the requested 100 without relying on the X leads.
- **youtube-full used:** ten search pages returned 250 hits / 246 unique video IDs. Tutorials, reviews and unrelated hits were filtered; raw search hits were not counted as viable candidates merely to meet the quota.
- **Cross-platform discovery:** YouTube, Vimeo, Wistia, brand launch pages, creator/studio portfolios and an attributed X launch index. This is a broad search, not an exhaustive ranking of the entire internet. TikTok and Instagram were not systematically searched in this pass.
- **Eight automated audiovisual previews** used actual video/audio with the watch-video skill at 6 fps, limited to the stated clips. Seven of those films made the twenty; Arc was screened out. These are model-produced observations, not a claim that every film was personally watched in full.
- **Twelve caption tracks returned from fifteen requested videos.** HomePod’s repost, Notion Mail and the new Notion AI had no English captions available. Bulk metadata came back null, so view counts come from search/API snapshots and identified sources, not from that caption batch. Two follow-up metadata checks corroborated the original Dollar Shave Club and Slack uploads, displaying rounded counts of 29M and 1.4M respectively.
- **Exactly twenty selected** for complementary roles. Preference order: relevance to the person/environment thesis; convincing transition to a product; potential for a distinct visual idea; useful pace/proof mechanics; adaptable production; public reach evidence. This is an editorial ranking, not a numerical performance model.

## Why these twenty survived the cut

### 1. Pinterest — Disco Treehouse

[Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hehaJGC9NJQ) · Ledger C016 · AV preview + captions

**Evidence:** The automated preview shows two tastes—retro and forestcore—merging into one room. At 00:08.333–00:10.500 the shared board names their combined identity; at 00:10.500–00:25.200 digital selections cause physical furnishings to appear. This is unusually close to the spatial-personality brief.

**What to borrow — inference:** Make personality visible through objects, color and behavior before explaining it. Keep the same room and people on screen when the product enters, so the product continues the story.

**Deep-research question:** Track the match between each interface action, sound accent and physical change. Compare the 31-second and 15-second edits.

**Limit / adaptation:** Its instant physical transformations are advertising metaphors. ReSpace must distinguish a proposed design from furniture actually arriving. Reach differs sharply by edit; neither count establishes organic virality.

**Production judgment:** Medium to high: one controlled room and locked camera can simplify the idea; polished compositing and styling still matter.

Sources: [Source 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hehaJGC9NJQ) · [Source 2](https://www.behance.net/gallery/184282679/Pinterest-Its-Possible-Disco-Treehouse) · [Source 3](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sean-doyle_disco-treehouse-its-possible-pinterest-activity-7112327221880463360-l2Ul)

## What the reach evidence does and does not tell us

The clearest high-view YouTube references in the screened pool include [Dollar Shave Club](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUG9qYTJMsI) at about 29.03M; [Airbnb, stay together for less](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAf1_23Ihmw) at 18.05M; [Pinterest, Tropical Getaway, 15 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gI0cLpUDf1U) at 11.10M; [Pinterest, Disco Treehouse, 15 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EOmNL_5utbo) at 10.24M; [Lamborghini Revuelto](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sitXeGjm4Mc) at 8.94M; [the new Notion AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S92KX8-Hmlc) at 8.18M; and [Notion Mail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=54l-1NfXnX8) at 5.99M. These are observed indexed counts, not a global top list, and publication ages differ.

The X index reports much larger numbers for some posts, including 69.28M for 1X and 11.12M for rabbit. Those are **third-party X post-view figures**, not independently verified video plays. They remain discovery leads in the ledger, with [Infinite’s dataset attribution](https://infinite.fast/startup-launch-videos). They were not mixed into the YouTube ranking.

Public views alone do not reveal paid distribution, completion rate, qualified attention, sign-ups or whether the launch caused business growth. That is why a low-view but highly relevant film such as Wispr Flow can rank above a high-view but less transferable luxury launch. No repeatable recipe for extreme virality can be inferred from this sample.

## Creative principles worth testing for ReSpace

1. **Open with a visible human contradiction.** Pinterest makes different tastes tangible; Wispr dramatizes friction. For ReSpace, a promising hypothesis is a person whose life has changed while their room has not. The audience should recognize the mismatch before hearing an abstract explanation of spatial personality.
2. **Make the product answer the opening.** The reMarkable caption arc and Slack’s preview both provide a clear reason for the product to appear. Preserve the same person, room and unmet need across the ReSpace reveal; a sudden detour into features would break that thread.
3. **Put the proof in the physical setting.** IKEA Place shows how phone and room can remain legible together. ReSpace can show the actual scan, a concrete user intention, a proposed layout and identifiable real products. Any rendered proposal should read as a proposal; a filmed furnished-room payoff should be distinguishable from software output.
4. **Create changes of pace.** Slack’s interruption of overload and Wispr’s slower intervention make the faster parts more meaningful. “Fast-paced” should mean frequent useful changes—surprise, discovery, proof, relief—with enough time to see the result.
5. **End on life in the room.** HomePod and the home-oriented campaign premises suggest a stronger payoff than a floating interface: someone can now host, concentrate, make something or feel represented by the space. This is a creative hypothesis, not a claim that redesign produces a measured psychological outcome.

A useful narrative spine for the next phase is **human identity → a concrete mismatch in the room → an imagined possibility → ReSpace as the bridge → credible product proof → a human payoff**. This is a working research hypothesis, not a finished script or a decision to copy any one film. The full launch and shorter social edits can be planned after the deep viewing pass.
