# Launch-film editing style profile and effect library

**80 specific editing devices and effect variants · 32 cross-film fundamentals · 20 reference films**  
Research revision: 18 October 2026. This is a reusable editing reference, independent of the current ReSpace script.

The strongest common pattern is **controlled change**: a clear subject, action or visual anchor lets the film become surprising without becoming unreadable. Novelty comes from changing the right thing—surface, scale, medium, relationship, sound density or the meaning of a frame—while preserving enough continuity to understand the result. Fast cutting is one tool in that system.

This profile contains both elaborate effects and economical editorial devices. A perspective tunnel, a sentence-slot animation and a delayed wide reveal are different production tools; all can be creatively useful. **The 80 entries are not claimed to be 80 unrelated VFX algorithms.** Related variants are grouped and cross-referenced so the library is useful rather than inflated. Descriptions of intended feelings are editorial interpretations, not measured audience responses or guarantees of views.

## How to use the profile

1. Name the job of the moment: establish, surprise, compare, explain, expand, embody, resolve or invite.
2. Choose a style family that fits the surrounding scene. Select one main device and, at most, a supporting text or sound device for that beat.
3. Open the linked source and inspect the specified interval. The timestamps locate evidence; they are **not a frame-accurate edit decision list**.
4. Read **Observed** separately from **Recreation**. The first describes the reference; the second proposes a way to achieve a related effect with new assets. A recipe does not identify the original production method.
5. Build a rough version that proves the before/action/after. Add polish only once the transition communicates at normal playback.
6. Keep the result or reaction long enough to register. The effect is unfinished until the destination makes sense.

Use the smallest production method that preserves the idea. A matched hard cut can be stronger than an elaborate morph; an actor’s eye-line can sell a composite better than another particle layer. Exact durations, lens choices, plugins, sound stems and easing curves are not recoverable from sampled footage unless explicitly verified.

## Style families

| Family | Visual grammar | Typical emotional register | Best uses | What breaks it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spatial fantasy | Recognizable room; one impossible local change; growing depth or transformed surfaces; human reaction | Wonder, possibility, release | A world responding to a choice or feeling | Changing all geography at once; no contact or reaction |
| Comic live action | Ordinary scene; selectively withheld information; absurd prop or event; short afterbeat | Surprise, familiarity, confidence | Humanizing a frustrating task or tool | Explaining the joke; cutting before recognition |
| Precise interface choreography | Clear input; stable artifact; limited moving region; readable result; restrained chapter changes | Competence, ease, agency | Demonstrating actual operations | Zooming on every click; illegible text; fake continuity |
| Expressive graphic metaphor | Shape or motif persists across media; abstract concept gains behavior; a concrete return | Curiosity, tension, imaginative breadth | Giving form to invisible processes | Unrelated spectacle with no interpretable destination |
| Tactile cinematic footage | Hands, surfaces, material details, environmental light, purposeful wide views | Intimacy, care, credibility | Making digital or physical products feel part of life | Generic stock inserts; fake material sounds everywhere |
| Rhythmic montage | Repeated framing/action/type; selected variations; musical phrase; clear ending state | Energy, scope, participation | Variants, users, environments, repeated known tasks | Every shot changes both grammar and subject; no endpoint |

These families can coexist. Change families at a narrative turn or follow a shared object, gesture or shape across the boundary. Do not alternate styles arbitrarily just to keep adding novelty. The problem and solution can deliberately use different densities, but each should have an internally consistent grammar.

## 32 shared fundamentals

These are recurring constructions across the corpus, not rules followed by every film. Each principle includes multiple examples. The operational advice is a creative synthesis of those observations.
### F01 — Internal motion can carry pace while geography stays stable

Creative inference: Perceived activity need not require a new camera angle. Repeated changes within a known frame can provide rapid development while preserving the subject’s relationship to the environment.

**Apply:** Choose one stable anchor, introduce changes in distinct waves, and let each wave alter a meaningful state. Increase activity only after the viewer can locate the subject and the destination.

**Across films:**

- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:10.500–00:25.167: Furniture, wall treatment and a rug change around a centrally anchored couple.
- [Slack — So Yeah, We Tried Slack](deep/rank-07-slack-so-yeah/report.md), 00:29.833–00:35.667: Notifications multiply around a worker in one recognizable desk composition.

**Limit / failure:** A static camera alone does not make dense layers readable; overlapping changes still require hierarchy.

### F02 — An effect should change a state, not merely decorate a cut

Creative inference: The most useful flourishes visibly complete an operation or dramatize a consequence. They have a before and an after that can be described independently of their style.

**Apply:** For every effect, write its initial state, trigger and resulting state. If removing the effect changes no information or feeling, consider simplifying it.

**Across films:**

- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:08.250–00:09.125: Separate boards resolve into one shared board.
- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 00:52.167–00:56.500: The earlier difficult sentence becomes successful text output.
- [reMarkable — Get Your Brain Back](deep/rank-02-remarkable-get-your-brain-back/report.md), 01:41.000–01:41.750: A recognizable facial profile becomes divided fragments.

**Limit / failure:** This is an editorial heuristic; purely decorative movement can be appropriate, especially in a branded interlude.

### F03 — Return to the same problem when demonstrating the solution

Creative inference: A repeated situation reduces setup work and makes the result comparable. The viewer recognizes why the operation matters before a new explanation is required.

**Apply:** Capture reusable locations, object positions and task identifiers. Repeat the meaningful variable while changing the result; exact frame matching is optional.

**Across films:**

- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 00:06.333–00:35.200; 00:52.167–01:14.667: Spelling, tone and a name recur as problems and successful demonstrations.
- [Slack — So Yeah, We Tried Slack](deep/rank-07-slack-so-yeah/report.md), 00:29.833–00:35.667; 00:37.833–00:44.200: The same workplace shifts from crowded messages to organized communication.
- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:00.000–00:01.000; 00:25.167–00:28.400: The couple inhabit the room first as a blank space and later as a finished social setting.

**Limit / failure:** Wispr’s repeated settings are established, but claims of frame-identical camera angles were rejected.

### F04 — Keep one human anchor while the surrounding information changes

Creative inference: A continuing person or relationship can organize a dense explanation. The person supplies scale, reaction and continuity while graphics, locations or supporting characters change.

**Apply:** Preserve the person’s screen position, eye-line, route or vocal identity across changes. Choose which of those anchors remains constant before introducing visual novelty.

**Across films:**

- [Dollar Shave Club](deep/rank-09-dollar-shave-club/report.md), 00:44.800–00:51.000; 00:57.300–01:07.000: The presenter continues through packing and wagon stations.
- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:05.000–00:25.167: The seated couple remains central through interface and furnishing changes.
- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 00:52.167–01:14.667: The same user remains present while dictation solves several tasks.

**Limit / failure:** A recurring face is not a substitute for readable product actions; let unfamiliar operations occupy their own view.

### F05 — Density contrast makes a transition meaningful

Creative inference: A visual reset is strongest when the next sequence follows a different organizing rule. Fewer objects, slower motion or a tactile sound field can make the product transition feel like the answer to the preceding problem.

**Apply:** Define the outgoing and incoming density in layers, motion and sound. Remove specific pressures at the transition instead of merely adding a logo or changing music.

**Across films:**

- [reMarkable — Get Your Brain Back](deep/rank-02-remarkable-get-your-brain-back/report.md), 02:03.500–02:22.799: Dense animated danger gives way to a drawn-eye bridge and tactile device use.
- [Slack — So Yeah, We Tried Slack](deep/rank-07-slack-so-yeah/report.md), 00:35.833; 00:37.833–00:44.200: A clean identity card separates crowded notifications from orderly workplace graphics.

**Limit / failure:** The emotional relief is an interpretation, not a measured physiological or retention result; precise reMarkable live-action boundary remains uncertain.

### F06 — Physical reactions connect impossible graphics to the scene

Creative inference: An actor’s body can establish that an effect occupies the same space. Posture, gaze, reaching and avoidance give a composited event weight even when its occurrence is clearly fantastical.

**Apply:** Rehearse the reaction to a timed cue, record clean plates when needed, and preserve contact/occlusion relationships. Let the performer react to one event at a time.

**Across films:**

- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:21.000–00:23.500: The couple raises their feet as the rug unfurls.
- [Slack — So Yeah, We Tried Slack](deep/rank-07-slack-so-yeah/report.md), 00:29.833–00:35.667: The worker reacts as message graphics overwhelm the desk area.

**Limit / failure:** These films do not establish the original compositing or prop fabrication method; reconstruction instructions are proposals.

### F07 — Match a sound accent to a visible cause

Creative inference: An audible click, impact or buzz becomes informative when the viewer can identify the action that caused it. Different materials and outcomes can have different sonic signatures.

**Apply:** Place the accent on the perceptual contact or state change, not automatically on every cut. Distinguish input, processing and result sounds when those stages need explanation.

**Across films:**

- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:10.500–00:24.500: Saving is paired with digital acknowledgements and physical-sounding room changes.
- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 00:07.125–00:20.125: Error buzzers interrupt failed typed entries.
- [Dollar Shave Club](deep/rank-09-dollar-shave-club/report.md), 00:51.000–00:54.200: Swishing and impacts accompany the prop disruption.

**Limit / failure:** Exact original sources and frame synchronization were not independently measured; use the relationships, not unsupported instrument names.

### F08 — Let the product enter through an established action

Creative inference: A motivated handoff from story to product maintains continuity. A character offers a tool, a familiar motif becomes its material, or an existing choice changes the room.

**Apply:** Identify the action or image already carrying the story, then make the first product view answer it. Separate the conceptual turn, brand introduction and functional proof if they occur at different moments.

**Across films:**

- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 00:43.167–00:45.500: The therapist presents the software on a tablet within the counseling scene.
- [reMarkable — Get Your Brain Back](deep/rank-02-remarkable-get-your-brain-back/report.md), 02:03.500–02:11.999: The eye motif leads into drawing on the product surface.

**Limit / failure:** A theatrical transformation should not be presented as literal software behavior unless that behavior is demonstrated.

### F09 — A joke needs readable setup, interruption and afterbeat

Creative inference: Comedy is an editing relationship, not simply an eccentric prop. Withholding information, punctuating a routine action and allowing a response give the gag a shape.

**Apply:** Establish the ordinary action first, isolate the surprising beat, and keep a reaction or short hold before moving on. Avoid placing crucial UI reading over the punchline.

**Across films:**

- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 00:00.000–00:07.125: A familiar counseling setup precedes the wider oversized-keyboard reveal.
- [Dollar Shave Club](deep/rank-09-dollar-shave-club/report.md), 00:51.000–00:57.300: Packing-table disruption is followed by a mascot beat.
- [Slack — So Yeah, We Tried Slack](deep/rank-07-slack-so-yeah/report.md), 00:26.333–00:29.833: A closet encounter interrupts the interview account before returning to commentary.

**Limit / failure:** Returned times are navigation aids; precise comedic pause lengths need checking in an editing timeline.

### F10 — Finish the metaphor before the final identity card

Creative inference: A visual idea carries more coherence when its characters, objects or motifs receive a conclusion. The closing brand card then follows a completed action rather than cutting off an unresolved story.

**Apply:** List the film’s distinctive recurring elements and choose one concise payoff action. Resolve it, then simplify composition and reading load for the destination.

**Across films:**

- [Wispr — Break Up With Your Keyboard](deep/rank-03-wispr-break-up-keyboard/report.md), 01:14.833–01:17.000: The keyboard character moves out after successful proof.
- [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](deep/rank-01-pinterest-disco-treehouse/report.md), 00:25.167–00:28.400: The couple celebrates in the room they have shaped.
- [Dollar Shave Club](deep/rank-09-dollar-shave-club/report.md), 01:16.000–01:22.000: Recurring participants join a warehouse celebration before the identity/website close.

**Limit / failure:** Do not introduce a new narrative premise in the finale; spectacle should remain connected to the established world.

### F11 — Use sparse typography as a density reset between proofs

The contrast is between information-dense interaction and a short, spacious proposition. A title creates reading room and identifies the next job before the viewer encounters its controls.

**Apply:** Cut away from detailed UI at a natural completion. Put one benefit on a quiet field; reveal the next interaction only after that phrase is readable. Maintain the same type system across cards.

**Check:** If the title is removed, does the viewer lose the next task? If not, it may be redundant; if it is necessary, can it be read at normal playback?

**Across films:**

- [Notion Mail](deep/rank-10-notion-mail/report.md), 00:26.333–00:33.000: Sparse drafting card precedes composer/snippet interaction.
- [Notion AI](deep/rank-20-notion-ai/report.md), 00:21.000–00:31.000: Writing chapter title precedes dense highlighted-document editing.
- [Granola — Original Launch Sizzle](deep/rank-17-granola/report.md), 00:41.000–00:49.000: The document demonstration gives way to typography and meeting-type variation.

**Limit / failure:** The examples do not prescribe universal card durations; reading load and soundtrack change the timing.

### F12 — Make the recurring transition device belong to the product world

A recurring gesture carries identity most coherently when its material or action comes from the subject: a mail airplane, text caret, pixel screen or editing operation. This is a specific selection criterion for motifs, not a requirement that every cut be branded.

**Apply:** Inventory three physical or interface actions native to the product. Choose one readable at small size. Develop two or three applications—entry, chapter boundary, close—without forcing it into every scene.

**Check:** Could the exact motif serve any unrelated company without alteration? If yes, the connection is probably decorative.

**Across films:**

- [Notion Mail](deep/rank-10-notion-mail/report.md), 00:00.000–00:06.000: The paper airplane returns across introductory graphic cards.
- [Granola — Original Launch Sizzle](deep/rank-17-granola/report.md), 00:00.000–00:04.000;00:49.000–00:51.583: Typing/caret identity opens the film and reappears at the close.

**Limit / failure:** The inference concerns coherence, not trademark ownership or demonstrated conversion.

### F13 — Slow down for unfamiliar mechanics; accelerate recognizable repetition

Fast films do not teach every operation at their fastest pace. They hold the first causal example, then compress repetitions, variants or a recap after the visual grammar is established.

**Apply:** Mark the first unfamiliar step in the cut. Give it a complete before/action/result. Reserve quick repetitions for examples the viewer can classify from silhouette, familiar structure or prior explanation.

**Check:** Watch without knowing the script: can you explain the unfamiliar step before the montage begins?

**Across films:**

### F14 — Change viewing scale to follow the task, not to decorate every click

These films move between context, a readable action area and a result. Scale change is purposeful when each new crop answers a different question: where are we, what happened, what improved?

**Apply:** Build a three-scale coverage plan before recording. Preserve one recognizable landmark through any digital push. Reframe only when the next detail would otherwise be too small to read.

**Check:** Freeze before and after each zoom: does the new crop reveal necessary information, or merely make the same information larger?

**Across films:**

### F15 — Keep concurrent panels in a clear attention hierarchy

Simultaneous panes work when they give different kinds of information and one is quieter. Context can persist in one pane while an action develops in another; two competing demonstrations are harder to follow.

**Apply:** Define which panel the viewer must read and which provides context. Give the reading panel sufficient area and pause unnecessary motion in the other. Align panel changes with handoffs of attention.

**Check:** Ask a viewer where they looked first; if the decoration beats the important action, reduce its size, contrast or motion.

**Across films:**

- [Granola — Original Launch Sizzle](deep/rank-17-granola/report.md), 00:20.000–00:29.000: Meeting grid remains beside the notepad and activity indicators.
- [Notion AI](editing-audit/rank-20-clip-1.txt), 00:12.375–00:21.000: Chat occupies the left while a blue mascot panel supplies character on the right.
- [Descript — Damn Jenny](deep/rank-04-descript/report.md), 02:40.600–02:43.000: Distinct color-coded cast columns support a shared closing exchange.

**Limit / failure:** The Descript ensemble has a different job from a UI split screen; the commonality is allocation, not identical layout.

### F16 — Use color as a location or chapter identifier

A limited set of consistent color fields makes rapid returns legible. Color identifies a person, workspace or task, allowing the edit to change content without making the audience reorient from zero.

**Apply:** Assign a stable color role before building transitions. Keep that role across wide, close and composite shots. Change the field when the task or context genuinely changes, rather than cycling colors arbitrarily.

**Check:** Can the chapter or subject still be recognized in a blurred thumbnail?

**Across films:**

### F17 — Return to a person or usable artifact after abstract spectacle

An expressive graphic can dramatize a benefit, but these examples reconnect it with a reaction, usable result or real interaction. That return gives the imaginative passage an intelligible destination.

**Apply:** For each stylized insert, write the concrete result it returns to. Shoot or capture that result first. Keep the abstract passage only as long as it adds meaning or feeling.

**Check:** Does the sequence leave the viewer with something inspectable, or only the feeling that an effect happened?

### F18 — Give impossible motion a visible physical initiator

Introduce the effect through a recognizable gesture or operation before expanding its consequences. The initiating contact gives viewers a causal anchor even when the following action is surreal. It need not imply a real product capability.

**Apply:** Choose one hand contact, tap or movement. Establish its target, delay the larger change until that action reads, then show the affected area.

**Check:** Can a viewer point to what started the change after watching without narration?

**Across films:**

- [HomePod — Welcome Home](deep/rank-05-homepod/report.md), 01:13.000–01:17.000: A hand reaches toward the table before its surface elongates.
- [IKEA Kreativ — Home Office](deep/rank-06-ikea-kreativ/report.md), 00:03.000–00:08.667: Phone use and targeted room graphics precede the larger rearrangement.

**Limit / failure:** The initiation may be a film metaphor; do not let an edit make an unsupported product operation look real.

### F19 — Treat arrival and settling as part of the effect

Movement earns satisfaction through a destination and a small period of resolution. Floating possessions entering storage and interface activity reaching completion both close a visible loop. Motion without a destination is decoration.

**Apply:** Storyboard the finished state first, then animate toward it. Include deceleration, contact or confirmation, followed by enough stillness to recognize completion.

**Check:** Does the audience know what finished and where the moving element ended?

**Across films:**

- [Tylko — Bedroom](deep/rank-11-tylko/report.md), 00:00.000–00:06.000: Floating possessions move into storage destinations and settle.
- [Dropbox — Creative Freedom](deep/rank-15-dropbox/report.md), 00:47.333–00:54.000: The recurring circle resolves into a completion/synchronization role.

**Limit / failure:** Do not insert fake completion or exaggerated bouncing merely to make an animation feel satisfying.

### F20 — Create variety by changing one property at a time

Stable form, framing or performer makes prop, color or medium changes easy to register. Constrained variation can produce more impact than changing camera, lighting and content simultaneously.

**Apply:** Choose an invariant: position, silhouette, person or shape. Change the one variable the shot is meant to celebrate.

**Check:** Can the viewer name what changed without watching twice?

**Across films:**

- [IKEA Kreativ — Home Office](deep/rank-06-ikea-kreativ/report.md), 00:00.000–00:01.333: The performer stays in related framing while the held hobby prop changes.
- [Tylko — Bedroom](deep/rank-11-tylko/report.md), 00:12.833–00:14.333: Furniture silhouette remains recognizable while finishes change.
- [Dropbox — Creative Freedom](deep/rank-15-dropbox/report.md), 00:00.000–00:12.333: A geometric motif persists across different media and objects.

**Limit / failure:** This is not a demand for all shots to match; use stable variation where comparison or accumulation is the point.

### F21 — Use recurring material to connect different narrative states

Paper, packaging, furniture and other physical materials carry continuity across distinct shot types and narrative states. Sonos connects moving-home packaging with product handling; Dropbox turns a drawing into a physical form; HomePod transforms a familiar table after the product has already entered. The commonality is material continuity, not that all three introduce the product at that moment.

**Apply:** Find a material already present in the story and give it a consequential later role: packaging becomes an installation action, a drawing becomes a shape, or a surface becomes responsive. Record the material’s texture, movement and contact.

**Check:** Can the viewer recognize the recurring material and explain what its role has changed into?

**Across films:**

- [Sonos — Music Makes It Home](deep/rank-12-sonos/report.md), 00:15.000–00:21.000: Moving-home packaging and speaker unpacking share a tactile material vocabulary.
- [Dropbox — Creative Freedom](deep/rank-15-dropbox/report.md), 00:19.167–00:22.500: The technical drawing is crumpled into a new physical shape.
- [HomePod — Welcome Home](deep/rank-05-homepod/report.md), 01:13.000–01:17.000: A familiar tabletop becomes the surface of the first impossible deformation.

**Limit / failure:** A shared material alone is not a causal link; keep the relationship understandable and avoid invented functional claims.

### F22 — Follow an input through output to human response

A device action is only one link. Showing the affected object or state, then a person’s response, makes the operation readable and restores the human premise. The response can be subtle.

**Apply:** Build a short input→output→response sequence. Remove intervening screens that do not clarify the cause.

**Check:** Can the audience connect the response to a visible change rather than a voiceover promise?

**Across films:**

- [Sonos — Music Makes It Home](deep/rank-12-sonos/report.md), 00:18.833–00:28.000: Phone music selection is followed by speaker imagery and listener responses across households.
- [IKEA Kreativ — Home Office](deep/rank-06-ikea-kreativ/report.md), 00:04.167–00:12.167: The phone preview is followed by the person’s reaction and a wider redesigned room.

**Limit / failure:** Cross-household montage does not prove one continuous event. Do not imply measurable emotional improvement from a staged reaction.

### F23 — Let the environment carry personality through use and belongings

The films repeatedly make rooms legible through meaningful objects or human activities. Character can be established before explanatory language, and furniture can gain purpose through what it holds or enables.

**Apply:** Choose props or actions that imply an actual routine. Preserve them when moving from problem to result.

**Check:** Would these possessions or actions make sense for this specific person rather than any generic lifestyle ad?

**Across films:**

- [IKEA Kreativ — Home Office](deep/rank-06-ikea-kreativ/report.md), 00:00.000–00:03.000: Hobby props introduced close are revealed as belongings competing with a desk activity.

- [Sonos — Music Makes It Home](deep/rank-12-sonos/report.md), 00:28.000–00:40.000: Cooking, cleaning and other domestic routines gain visible movement after music enters the sequence.

**Limit / failure:** Avoid treating all possessions as clutter; the film can organize personality without erasing it.

### F24 — Use an overhead view to reveal relationships that eye-level hides

An overhead composition can turn separate objects into a readable system: component spacing, destinations and the human route become visible together. Floyd uses a person inside an organized parts field; Tylko makes small objects and storage destinations legible from above. The useful commonality is the relational view, not overhead photography as a decorative style.

**Apply:** Choose the relationship before choosing the angle. Lay out parts or destinations with enough separation to read. Include a hand or person only if it adds scale or demonstrates a route; preserve the layout long enough to inspect.

**Check:** Can the viewer see a relationship in this angle that the previous angle could not communicate?

**Across films:**

- [E050](#e050) — Tylko — Bedroom, 00:06.000–00:10.000.
- [E073](#e073) — Floyd — The Floyd Ethos, 00:58.667–01:03.000; 01:03.000–01:17.500.

**Limit / failure:** A top view flattens height and can conceal depth; pair it with a side view when vertical fit matters.

### F25 — Separate the element that changes from the element that provides reading continuity

IKEA Place holds one word while locations change; Notion AI holds sentence structure while capability pills swap; Daylight keeps a recurring text treatment across sensory images. These are related but different allocation strategies. Decide whether the viewer is comparing pictures, reading variants or following an argument, then stabilize the complementary layer.

**Apply:** Mark one changing layer and one reading anchor in the storyboard. Keep position, baseline and contrast dependable. If both text and picture must change together, reduce the text to a form that can be recognized quickly.

**Check:** With the sound muted, is it obvious which element is meant to be compared and which is meant to remain familiar?

### F26 — Resolve a visual world into a mark that belongs to it

Headspace contracts its inhabited park into a circle, Dropbox gives its recurring circular motif a completion role, and Granola builds identity out of a typing gesture. The shared principle is a traceable relationship between the film’s image language and its ending symbol. The exact shape or transition should differ with the subject.

**Apply:** Choose a shape or action already present in the film. Plan the final clean state first, then a short bridge that retains a recognizable feature from the outgoing world. Remove secondary detail as the mark becomes smaller.

**Check:** Can someone describe the connection between the story image and the final mark without reading the brand name?

### F27 — Restore human scale after isolated product beauty shots

Playdate places its isolated hardware into a real hand; Floyd lets a person move through components; Daylight returns from screen details to a wider shared room. Each sequence provides a physical reference after abstraction or isolation. Scale is not only a measurement: handling shows the relation between a product and a body.

**Apply:** Pair at least one isolated object view with a hand, body or known environmental reference. Match the relevant orientation so the viewer can connect the two. Let the contact finish before introducing another camera move.

**Check:** Can the viewer imagine holding, reaching or moving around the object after the sequence?

### F28 — Music can connect cuts without dictating every visual change

The IKEA domestic montage, IKEA Place’s repeated object/location cuts and Headspace’s scene changes preserve musical continuity while the picture changes structure. This makes a continuous phrase available as the spine of a sequence. It does not establish a universal requirement to cut on every beat or to restart music for every chapter.

**Apply:** Lay out the visual causal sequence first, then find a musical phrase that can hold it. Choose a few strong synchronization points and allow other cuts to follow actions, reading or reactions. Preserve phrase continuity where locations change but the idea continues.

**Check:** Does the sequence still make sense when the music is muted, and does the music still phrase naturally without the picture?

### F29 — Sound density can change the meaning of an image without simply becoming louder

reMarkable’s tactile product passage thins the sensory field after animated pressure; Wispr gives failed typing an intrusive buzzer; Descript makes deletion rhythmic. These are different sound roles: relief, obstruction and agency. Their commonality is a designed relationship between the audible texture and the state of the task.

**Apply:** Define what each stage should sound like in terms of event count, texture and foreground/background priority. Reserve harshness for a meaningful interruption. Let a successful action have a clearer or sparser sound rather than automatically adding a bigger impact.

**Check:** Can the sound-only cut distinguish problem, action and resolution without relying on spoken explanation?

### F30 — A wider view should change the meaning of the detail

Wispr’s wide reveal exposes the absurd partner, IKEA Kreativ turns hobby props into a space conflict, and Daylight reveals that separate readers share one sofa. A wide shot is most useful when it supplies a missing relationship. Merely showing more pixels is less consequential than revealing who or what the earlier image excluded.

**Apply:** Storyboard the information deliberately withheld by the close view. Place the missing person or object where it will be immediately readable in the wide. Cut after curiosity has formed but before the setup becomes confusing.

**Check:** Complete the sentence: the wide reveals that the earlier detail was actually part of ___.

### F31 — When comparison matters, make the invariant visible

Daylight brings contrasting illumination into a shared sofa frame, Tylko holds furniture form while finishes vary, and IKEA Place replaces objects within one room anchor. The stable context supplies the reference for comparison. Simultaneous comparison and successive replacement are alternatives, chosen according to what must be compared.

**Apply:** Identify the variable under discussion and keep irrelevant conditions steady. For color/material use consistent lighting; for layouts use recognizable geometry; for workflows retain the same input. Show the comparison at a scale where the difference is actually visible.

**Check:** Can a viewer identify both the changed property and the conditions that remained constant?

### F32 — Give the ending a reading job after its movement job

Granola resolves its graphic identity, Notion AI returns its character before the closing lockup, and Floyd holds a title over moving shadows. The end can retain life, but the demanding transition finishes before the viewer must absorb identity or a destination. This is separate from resolving the story: the final layout still needs time to be read.

**Apply:** End the major movement, simplify the field, then hold only the necessary identity and action. Read the actual wording at ordinary playback and test it at the smallest expected viewing size. Subtle environmental motion can continue if it does not compete.

**Check:** Can a first-time viewer name the product and remember the requested next action without pausing?

### E003 — Wallpaper travels along the room rather than appearing as a flat card

**Family:** Environment-following wipe. **Verification:** fresh clip audit.

**Source:** [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hehaJGC9NJQ) · 00:16.375–00:20.875.  
**Version:** YouTube upload hehaJGC9NJQ

**Observed:** The fresh pass reports room paneling wrapping via scrolling wipe motion while shelves and plants accumulate from successive saved cards. Both support a surface-bound reveal; they do not establish one exact direction or a specific VFX technique throughout the whole range.

**Sound:** The prior full pass reports swishing or rolling texture for wall treatment and leaf rustle for plants; fresh output contains no audio observations.

**Purpose / feeling:** Creative inference: A conventional wipe becomes part of the represented world: decoration appears to cover actual architecture. The effect feels material and expansive because it respects corners and depth rather than sliding a rectangle over the entire film.

**Recreation — proposed method:**

1. Define the wall planes and corners before animating the reveal.
2. Create texture layers for each plane with a shared progression parameter.

5. Add a material-specific sound only while the covering advances; stop it when the surface settles.

**Assets:** Clean plate, wall textures, corner masks, foreground mattes, rolling or brushing sound.  
**Effort:** Moderate to high depending on camera movement and occlusion.  
**Failure mode:** A screen-wide linear wipe ignores architecture and resembles a presentation transition.  
**Useful for:** Wallpaper, paint, map coverage, building layers, data progressively filling a spatial surface.

### E004 — Rug rollout makes the actors move

**Family:** Body-coupled transformation. **Verification:** fresh clip audit.

**Source:** [Pinterest — Disco Treehouse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hehaJGC9NJQ) · 00:22.000–00:25.375.  
**Version:** YouTube upload hehaJGC9NJQ

**Observed:** A rug rolls into the floor area beneath the couple. The prior full-film pass describes both people raising their feet as it arrives; the fresh pass confirms the automatic rollout from under the sofa. Coffee-table choices then appear above the room before a selected table settles into the foreground. Treat the feet reaction and rollout as the central mechanism, separate from the later choice display.

**Sound:** Prior pass identifies rug friction and later table-settle accents. The fresh pass does not provide sound verification.

**Purpose / feeling:** Creative inference: The actors acknowledge the impossible event physically. This makes the effect playful and locates it in shared space; the transformation changes behavior instead of remaining an untouched overlay.

**Recreation — proposed method:**

1. Block the actors with a visible gap between their feet and the intended moving object.

<a id="production-notes"></a>
## Recreation parameters and capture requirements

The values in this section are **proposed starting points for a new edit**, not measurements of the reference films. Use them to build a rough test; adjust against actual reading, action and music. Do not transfer the source’s apparent speed without accounting for the complexity of your own image.

### Plan three moments for every device

**Recognition → change → resolution.** The viewer first needs to recognize the object or situation. The effect changes it. The result then needs enough stability to acquire meaning. Many unsuccessful transitions have plenty of middle and almost no beginning or ending.

For a first rough test on a **24 fps** timeline:

| Test component | Proposed starting range | What determines the final length |
|---|---|---|
| Establish a simple object before a swap | 12–24 frames | Whether silhouette, scale and placement are already familiar |
| Replace a known object or short label | A hard cut, or 4–8 frames of directed movement | A hard cut suits comparison; movement helps show a path or container |
| Hold a simple changed state | 12–36 frames | Visual complexity and whether this is the first example |
| Let a panel arrive | 8–16 frames | Travel distance, reading priority and the following action |
| Hold an unfamiliar UI result | Start with 36–72 frames | Actual amount of information the viewer needs to understand |
| Leave a reaction after a reveal | Start with 8–24 frames | Performance, joke, narration overlap and whether the reaction changes |
| Hold a short final identity / action | Start with 48–96 frames | Length of name, destination and action; test at delivery size |

These are test windows, not targets or minimums. A dramatic performance may need much longer; a known repeated shape may need less. For a different timeline frame rate, preserve intended duration rather than copying frame numbers. Do not speed up the voice to rescue an unreadable visual section.

### Six controls to specify before animating

1. **Invariant:** the thing that stays stable—person, baseline, floor anchor, silhouette, room edge or sound phrase.
2. **Changing region:** exactly what is allowed to move, replace, grow or disappear. Protect unrelated regions.
3. **Path:** start, destination and direction. Decide whether an element travels in screen space, along a surface or through scene depth.
4. **Velocity:** anticipation, acceleration, traversal, deceleration and final hold. A rigid object, paper sheet and interface panel should not all move with the same elastic bounce.
5. **Depth/contact:** what passes in front, what passes behind, what touches, and where shadows or reflections belong.
6. **Attention handoff:** when the viewer should stop looking at the initiating action and begin reading the result. Reduce activity in the old focus at that moment.

For an anchored swap, try a hard replacement before adding a morph. For a traveling reveal, animate the boundary in the target surface’s perspective. For a fast portal, ensure the next image inherits a recognizable position, contour or tonal region. For a comic reveal, remove unnecessary animation and test the cut against the performance.

### Capture requirements by effect family

| Family | Capture before leaving the set or app | Most expensive omission to fix later |
|---|---|---|
| Matched room or object states | Locked camera/lens/focus/exposure, floor marks, clean plate, state photographs, action handles | Drifting camera and changing shadows |
| Actor interacting with an imagined object | Timed cue, eye-line target, full gesture, clean background, foreground occlusion reference | A reaction that never acknowledges the object’s location |
| Surface change | Empty or unoccluded surface reference, texture detail, corner geometry, light/shadow reference | No usable plate behind the subject |
| Device screen replacement | Screen corners, tracking detail, realistic hand occlusion, reflection reference, actual interface capture | Finger crossings and glass reflections without a plan |
| UI choreography | Complete before/input/result, consistent data, cursor-free alternate if available, useful screen resolution | Only the animated flourish was recorded; no readable final result |
| Physical product drop / contact | Wide action, close contact, clean receiving hand, repeatable release, shadows | No convincing contact frame or changed hand posture |
| Comic scene | Tight setup, revealing wide, reaction, continuity for props and eye-lines | The only wide exposes the joke too early |
| Texture-led ending | Stable surface, subtle natural movement, space for final type, room tone | Background motion constantly crossing the letters |

A moving camera is not automatically more cinematic. Use it when the reveal depends on changing perspective, when following a person organizes the scene, or when parallax contributes to depth. Otherwise a stable plate gives the transformation more freedom and makes variants easier to compare.

### Sound design as a separate pass

Build the cut so the picture’s causal structure works before adding persuasive sound. Then organize the audio into **speech, music, action accents and environmental texture**. They can overlap, but they should not all demand foreground attention.

- **Input:** a click, touch, key or physical action can tell the viewer where agency begins. Use a sound only when there is an identifiable action.
- **Movement:** a short travel texture can connect departure and arrival. Avoid a long generic whoosh over a tiny screen move.
- **Contact:** the most important instant is often the object settling, a line landing on a word, or a state becoming final—not the start of the animation.
- **Result:** a reduced sound field, clean phrase ending or brief stable ambience can make completion feel different from processing.
- **Environment:** preserve enough room tone or material detail to stop live action from becoming a silent cutout floating above music.
- **Perspective:** a close macro may justify more audible handling detail; a wide exterior may call for less intimate sound. That is a proposed mix choice, not a verified rule in every reference.

Do three passes: picture without music, sound without picture, then the combined cut. A sound should reinforce the intended event rather than conceal a transition that does not make sense. Do not infer a music drop because a character hushes, or infer a literal needle-drop handoff because a turntable is shown.

### Typography and overlays

Give every text layer a job: **identity, navigation, action state, comparison, emphasis or subtitle**. If two layers perform the same job, remove one. If a layer requires reading, avoid placing its important change at the same moment as a new face, surprising prop or unfamiliar interface operation.

Use an existing baseline, margin or panel edge as the anchor. For changing labels, keep grammar and approximate length parallel. For a background montage with fixed text, choose each shot for contrast behind that exact text position; do not reposition the title shot by shot unless the movement itself carries meaning. For live-action composites, decide deliberately whether the graphic belongs to the screen or the room. A screen-space label should not accidentally behave like a badly tracked object.

When a panel expands to full screen, keep a shared identifier visible across the change. When it shrinks back beside a presenter, make the presenter’s new action or reaction worth returning to. Avoid performing a large scale transition merely because the narrator starts another sentence.

### Material and geometry checks

For spatial effects, review **contact shadows, occlusion, camera perspective, scale, reflections and exposure continuity** separately. A small error in any one of these can undermine an otherwise sophisticated animation. The viewer does not need to believe that a room really stretches, but they need to understand the rule of the imagined space.

For tabletop or architecture deformation, preserve an undeformed human or object as a scale reference. For floating possessions, plan destinations before paths and stagger the arrivals. For component layouts, make the arrangement meaningful rather than simply symmetrical. For a reflection becoming independent, establish correct reflection behavior first; otherwise there is no rule to break.

## Example combinations

These are **new editorial proposals assembled from the library**, not sequences claimed to appear in the source films. They are not additional entries toward the count of 80. Choose them for a suitable story; none is prescribed for ReSpace.

### 1. One choice changes the world

**E001 → E002 → E004 → E048.** Separate preferences converge, one selected surface changes, the person physically responds to an arriving object, and a reaction separates preview from a broader result. The progression moves from information to environment to body. Keep one initiating selection readable; otherwise the room changes feel arbitrary. The reaction should be restrained enough to belong to the scene.

### 2. Inside the thought, out into the tool

**E006 → E008 → E065 → E009.** A divided image becomes a recognizable motif; the camera follows that motif into a tangible drawing or display; the surrounding editing and sound become simpler. ### 3. A comic obstacle with a functional payoff

**E010 → E011 → E012 → E013.** Withhold the absurd source of the problem, give the failure a distinct audible interruption, revisit the same task after the product appears, then let the personified obstacle leave. ### 4. A capability becomes a visual rhythm

**E026 → E027 → E024.** First show one distributed change clearly, then one local substitution, then a more expressive rhythmic recap using the established action. ### 5. A product crosses from impossible to holdable

**E029 → E030 → E031.** Move through an abstracted product surface, reveal its human scale through a catch or hand contact, then connect one control gesture to related physical mechanisms and the real result. The hand and contact shadow are the credibility hinge. If the catch is weak, use a simple cut to handling instead.

### 6. A personal collection becomes order

**E045 → E049 → E050 → E052.** Establish a person through several possessions, suspend or mobilize those possessions, show clear destinations from above, then close the storage shell after revealing what it contains. ### 7. One action travels across a community

**E034 → E054 → E079 → E070.** A familiar event starts the workflow, a short causal relay demonstrates it, device-framed examples carry the behavior into different contexts, and a mosaic expands from one person to many. Keep the first demonstration long enough to understand. The later grid represents scope; it should not be required for reading detailed product output.

### 8. A repeated shape finds its purpose

**E057 → E058 → E059 → E060.** Carry a geometric form through different materials, let one material physically transform, use a scale-changing visual analogy, then give the motif a completion role. The final operational meaning makes the preceding visual play coherent. Avoid a dozen unrelated materials when three articulate the idea.

### 9. A quiet gesture releases a kinetic passage

**E061 → E062 → E063 → E055.** A lit window invites entry, an action creates a reaction elsewhere, a small quieting gesture creates visual counterpoint, and ordinary routines take on shared movement. A continuous musical phrase can bind the passage. Do not automatically mute the track at the hush; test both versions as separate creative choices.

### 10. A comparison becomes a broader invitation

**E067 → E068 → E078 → E064.** Reveal two contrasting states in shared space, articulate the useful distinction with a stable text system, extend the idea across locations, then resolve on one inhabited building or place. This scales an argument from particular to general. Keep comparative claims supported independently of the stylized lighting or montage.

### 11. A demo that retains a human presence

**E039 → E035 → E036 → E048.** A moving divider introduces the working interface beside a quieter human or character panel; the layout holds while the operation develops; the output grows in place; a short reaction closes the sequence. Only one pane should require close attention at a time. If both panels contain crucial reading, alternate full-screen views instead.

### 12. Spectacle returns to ordinary life

**E041 → E042 → E043 → E044 → E076.** A small tactile anomaly opens an expanding world, a reflection breaks its rule, and the film returns to a familiar resting place before a quiet textured end frame. The ordinary opening and ending are essential; without them the middle is an isolated effects reel. A low-budget adaptation can use one small deformation and a matched return rather than attempting the whole spatial sequence.

<a id="review-checklist"></a>
## Review checklist

Use this after the first assembly and again after polish. Each question should produce a concrete edit decision.

1. **Recognition:** What must the viewer understand before this effect begins? Is that information actually visible?
2. **Trigger:** Can the initiating action be located without the narration explaining it?
3. **Invariant:** What stays fixed while the change happens? Does it remain visible long enough to be useful?
4. **Destination:** Does the effect end in a meaningful state, or merely hand off to another transition?
5. **Layer priority:** At the busiest moment, which one thing should the viewer look at? Are motion and contrast supporting that choice?
6. **Reading:** Can necessary type and UI be read once at the intended output size, without pausing?
7. **Performance:** Does the actor’s eye-line, posture and reaction agree with the imagined event’s location and timing?
8. **Geometry:** Are contact, scale, perspective, shadows and foreground occlusion coherent?
9. **Continuity:** Is this a true match cut, a thematic association, a jump cut, a composite or a distant callback? Name it correctly in production notes.
10. **Sound:** Does each prominent accent have a visible cause? Are quiet and silent being confused?
11. **Rhythm:** Does the first unfamiliar operation receive more attention than subsequent recognizable repetitions?
12. **Contrast:** Is the payoff measurably simpler in layers or motion than the peak, or merely described as calm?
13. **Credibility:** Is a cut compressing time, a simulation imagining a result, or an actual operation being demonstrated? Does the sequence make that distinction clear where it matters?
14. **Originality:** Does the device grow out of this subject’s objects, actions and personality, or borrow another brand’s recognizable surface treatment?
15. **Restraint:** Which effect could be removed without losing meaning? Remove it and compare the two cuts.
16. **Ending:** Has the story resolved before the identity and next action need to be read?

Do not judge the reference solely by view counts or assume an effect caused its reach. No retention curves, controlled creative tests or causal performance data were available for this profile. Use the library to generate and test concrete edits, not as a claim that a specific transition guarantees virality.
