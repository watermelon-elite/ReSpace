**Did you implement a generative AI model or API in your hack this weekend?**  
**If so, how and why did you use it?**

Yes. ReSpace uses generative AI from two providers, each for the part it's best at: Google's Gemini plans the redesign, and OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol and gpt-image-2 rebuild and visualize the room. Our rule throughout: the models handle taste, language and appearance, and deterministic code handles geometry, facts and money.

**How we used it:**

1. **Understanding your request (Gemini).** You describe what you want in one sentence, like "a warm reading corner, under $600". Gemini works out whether that means adding a few things or a full redesign, picks up the style, and reads the budget. If you didn't give a budget, it asks. There's no mode picker to fill in.  
2. **Interview and shopping agent (Gemini).** Gemini then asks a few questions, one at a time, that you answer in your own words or skip: what you do in the room, who uses it, what matters most. When a choice matters, it shows real listings with photos and prices and explains the trade-off ("a better couch, or two extra chairs?"). It works through three tools: search\_products, ask\_user and finalize\_selection. Gemini only requests an action. Our Python/FastAPI backend runs each request one at a time, checks it, saves the result and then resumes the model. Gemini works from the room's real RoomPlan measurements, and every product it sees comes with verified dimensions, so it reasons about real sizes and a real budget. It can't raise your budget without your explicit approval.  
3. **Reading and ranking products (Gemini Flash).** Gemini reads merchant pages that a headless Playwright browser loads and pulls out labeled width, depth and height, telling item dimensions apart from package dimensions. It must quote where each number came from; code checks that the quote is really on the page and converts the units itself, and a product missing any dimension is never shown. Gemini also scores each product on how well it fits your use of the room and your style. Code adds the value score by comparing prices across similar products, so the model never judges price.  
4. **Spatial planning (Gemini).** Gemini outputs a structured plan of moves, removals and additions, guided by layout guidance we wrote from interior-layout research. Each item gets a priority and a named anchor that points at real object IDs, such as "against wall w2, centered", "under sofa o3" or "on top of side table o4". Gemini never outputs coordinates. Our Python solver turns each anchor into an exact position and checks collisions, door clearances and walkways. If an item doesn't fit, the solver drops the least important items first.  
5. **Natural-language edits (Gemini).** Type "swap the rug for something cheaper" or "bring back the lamp". Every edit is validated, the parts that succeed are saved as a new version you can undo, and anything that failed comes with a plain warning.  
6. **3D room walkthrough (OpenAI GPT-6 Sol).** We pick up to 64 sharp scan photos taken from different viewpoints and send them with a pooled LiDAR depth summary and a simplified room mesh. Sol lists everything that belongs in the room, then models the objects four at a time: shape, parts, materials and the hidden surfaces the camera never saw. It skips clutter like cleaning bottles and loose cables. Walls, floor and ceiling come from RoomPlan's measurements, not the model. Code validates every object's geometry before it enters the walkthrough, and every generation stops at a fixed $10 API budget.  
7. **Photorealistic image (gpt-image-2).** On request, we render one eye-level view from a corner of the room and send it to Sol with a few of the original scan photos. Sol calls gpt-image-2, which produces a photo-style image of the room with its current arrangement. You can share it.

We also used SPAR3D, an image-to-3D model, offline to turn IKEA product photos into the 3D models in our bundled meeting-room redesign demo.

**Why:** Redesigning a room is half taste and half geometry. Generative models are great at taste, intent, conversation and making things look real, but they're unreliable with measurements, coordinates and product facts. So every model has a narrow job and code checks its output. Gemini decides *what* belongs in the room and *roughly where*. Code verifies every dimension, computes every position and enforces the budget. Sol and gpt-image-2 make the room look and feel real, and measured walls and validation keep them honest. Gemini can't invent a measurement, fake a verification, place furniture through a wall or quietly raise your budget. The result is a design that fits your room, with real products and working Buy links.
