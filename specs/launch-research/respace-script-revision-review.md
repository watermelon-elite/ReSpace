# ReSpace script review and revision decisions

The first draft had a usable production plan and a clear imagined-future ending. Its main weakness was that it spent too long describing personalization before showing what made ReSpace personal. The revision keeps the preferred opening line and three-minute runtime, brings the app forward, and gives its questions and product choices more substance.

The revised [script and 43-shot storyboard](respace-launch-script-storyboard.md) is the production draft. The [recording copy](respace-launch-voiceover.txt) contains its 224 spoken words.

The new request for fuller product explanation relaxes the earlier approximate 60-word-per-minute average. The additional 52 words mostly explain relationships that were previously missing. At a conversational read, the film still has substantial room for interface inspection, sound and performance. The card-game sequence retains thirty seconds and several unvoiced beats.

## What makes the product passage more informative

The narration now distinguishes the inputs and their purposes:

- The scan establishes the actual room; Keep and budget establish constraints.
- The user describes the life the room should support.
- ReSpace creates questions around that room and those goals, clarifying details that have not already been supplied.
- Answers build a taste profile that guides materials, atmosphere and the proposed design.
- Real products appear alongside the existing furniture. A product card supplies size, price and a retailer destination.
- A specific natural-language edit produces a new design the viewer can compare.
- Community shows other people's ideas and gives the student somewhere to share his own proposal.

The generated-question wording does not promise that every answer necessarily creates a new branch. The filming session should supply the actual question text and outputs. A separate profile dashboard is unnecessary if the app does not expose one.

## Production choices retained

The film still starts in a barren dorm and ends in that same physical room. The furnished study and friends scenes are explicitly imagined, with real actors using the existing desk, chair and bed. The app itself displays the room proposal without people.

Technical, affiliate/freemium and future sections remain eight-second brainstorming reservations. Traction remains absent. Removing those reservations yields a 02:36 story cut.

The visual concept board still serves composition and mood; the revised written storyboard governs timing, dialogue and the card-game action.
