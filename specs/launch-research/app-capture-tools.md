# App capture and device-animation tools

Researched 2026-10-18 for a fast, zero-cost ReSpace launch-video workflow. These are production recommendations, not product requirements. **Confirmed** means supported by the linked first-party documentation; **Observed** means checked in the live editor; **Inferred** means a production judgement. The initial research below used no ReSpace footage; the subsequent authorized smoke tests used real Simulator screenshots and a navigation recording.

## Follow-up smoke tests

**Observed:** the [fifteen-case experiment](../../experiments/video-smoke-tests/README.md) retains ten usable MP4 samples and ten editable Recordly projects. Manual zooms, connected zooms, arrows, portrait framing and native 2× playback rendered successfully. A Recordly text overlay repeatedly produced a gray underlying screen. FreeMockup produced a verified HD 3D-phone clip; its 4K portrait render completed in the browser, but download verification failed. Continuous playback of an imported video texture remains unverified. Read the experiment's individual completion levels before relying on a treatment. No paid features or Compound imports were used in that experiment.

## freemockup.video: limits, commercial use, privacy and live check

- **Confirmed:** generated exports can be used for lawful personal or commercial projects; the underlying templates, models and assets cannot be extracted/redistributed as standalone resources. Uploaded content remains the user's property, with a limited service-operation license granted to the operator. [Terms §§5,7](https://www.freemockup.video/terms-of-service/)
- **Confirmed:** the homepage advertises transparent-background support in Free. Pro help distinguishes normal MP4 from transparent MOV and recommends short tests in a desktop Chromium browser for export failures. This does **not** verify which transparent codec is available in every Free browser. [Product](https://www.freemockup.video/), [export help](https://www.freemockup.video/help/pro/)
- **Observed:** in the anonymous Free editor, an iPhone 15 Pro scene and animation presets loaded. Export controls offered 1080×1920 or 2160×3840, each at 30fps; there was a transparent-background toggle. No login or payment prompt appeared. The scene visibly had phone sides, reflections and perspective. [Editor](https://www.freemockup.video/app/)
- **Observed:** a 10-second, 1080×1920/30fps MP4 export using only the site's blank placeholder screen started and reached 22% before the disposable test was closed. The export page warns that hidden tabs pause capture. Completion and the downloaded file were not verified; do not describe 4K, transparency, visual quality or rendering speed as export-tested.
- **Confirmed:** this is not a blanket offline/privacy promise. Its policy describes browser-local drafts, authenticated cloud projects, analytics, and automatic export reliability records even for anonymous users. Diagnostic records exclude uploaded media, project contents, filenames and credentials. [Privacy §§2–4](https://www.freemockup.video/privacy/)
- **Inferred:** make a five-second test using a disposable screen before committing a production sequence, and check the file in the target editor. Keep the export tab visible. For simple shots, a baked brand-color background avoids codec/alpha compatibility work.

## Automatic zooms and iPhone taps

- **Confirmed:** Recordly imports existing recordings, supports manual zoom regions, and suggests automatic zooms from cursor activity. Its current repository is AGPL 3.0; older forks/readmes may still say MIT. [Upstream README](https://github.com/webadderallorg/Recordly)
- **Inferred:** automatic cursor-following should not be mistaken for tap detection in a native iPhone recording. A pre-recorded video contains no reliable tap-coordinate metadata for a recorder to recover. Plan manual zooms for the two or three interface decisions the audience should read. An iPhone Mirroring/window capture may carry Mac cursor activity, but that is a different capture route and needs a trial.
- **Confirmed:** Screenize is an Apache-2.0 free macOS alternative with auto-zoom, timeline controls, MP4/MOV/GIF and advertised export up to 4K/240fps. Its author paused active development on May 22, 2026 and points users toward newer alternatives including Recordly. [Official README](https://github.com/syi0808/screenize/blob/main/README.md)
- **Inferred:** use Recordly as the first free screen-polish candidate; Screenize is a fallback if the native macOS workflow suits better. Neither's advertised export ceiling means a recorded iPhone source actually contains that many frames.

## Paid tools worth knowing, without building a free pipeline around trials

- **Confirmed:** Screen Studio at **screen.studio** offers USB iPhone/iPad capture, device framing, zooms and up to 4K/60fps export. Current listed pricing is $29 monthly or $9/month on the yearly plan. [Product/pricing](https://screen.studio/), [iPhone guide](https://screen.studio/guide/recording-iphone-ipad). **Inferred:** strong time-saving purchase if free tools prove awkward, but its device framing does not establish free-camera 3D animation support. No claim about current trial-export limits was verified.
- **Confirmed:** Rotato is purpose-built photorealistic 3D device animation, accepts screen images/video, and supports alpha-capable video and image sequences. Its unlimited Mac trial puts a watermark on exports until upgraded; paid Mac licenses are one-time with an update period. [Official product information](https://rotato.app/ai-info), [trial/watermark and commercial-use FAQ](https://rotato.app/generic), [pricing](https://rotato.app/pricing). **Inferred:** a paid speed upgrade when preset free mockups cannot match the shot.
- **Confirmed:** Jitter Free is limited to 720p/30fps; watermark-free export is listed as a Pro benefit. Eligible students/educators can apply for 4K/120fps, transparency and no watermark in personal Drafts. [Free limits](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/16761802-trial-jitter-for-free), [plan comparison](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/14809073-compare-jitter-s-subscription-plans), [education](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/6545337-apply-for-jitter-for-education). **Inferred:** useful for typography/motion design if already available, not the default free export route.

## Animockup and Shots: alternatives with evidence limits

- **Confirmed:** Animockup's MIT-licensed source supports images/video, 20+ device mockups, entry/exit animations, easing and export settings. It uses FabricJS and Anime.js and includes Paddle subscription code. The hosted website timed out during this research. [Author's repository](https://github.com/alyssaxuu/animockup)
- **Inferred:** this is a canvas/device-frame animation option rather than verified photorealistic 3D orbiting hardware. Open source does not prove that the hosted service offers unlimited free exports; current watermark, fps/resolution and commercial asset limits remain unverified. Self-hosting requires setup and is a poor speed-first choice here.
- **Confirmed:** Shots advertises animated mockups/video zoom. Its beta phone page claims no watermarks, free commercial use and MP4/GIF; PNG alone is explicitly labeled up to 4K. [Main product](https://shots.so/), [beta phone page](https://beta.shots.so/mockups/phone)
- **Inferred:** optional quick alternative for framed screenshots or parallax. Its current free video resolution/fps and true 3D-camera capabilities were not established, so the better-documented freemockup.video is the initial choice.

## Suggested production path

**Inferred production judgement:** record several short, clean real-device clips; use plain screen close-ups for readable app actions; render only two or three short 3D-phone shots with a consistent preset and background; deliver those rendered clips plus the clean masters to the existing main editor. Assemble the narration and timing before polishing device motion. Use Resolve only for a concrete missing edit, and Blender only for a shot the browser tool cannot produce.

**Inferred capture checklist:** disable distracting notifications, prepare repeatable demo data, hold the first/last UI state for a couple of seconds, move/tap deliberately, record two takes, and leave sufficient source resolution for planned crops. For actual RoomPlan/LiDAR actions, record on the supported real iPhone; use saved-room UI for shots that benefit from repeatability. Choose the film's delivery fps once and conform variable-rate capture if the editor drifts or stutters. Native source recording fps/resolution must be inspected in the actual file rather than assumed.

## Compound: installed app inspection

The intended product is [compound.mov](https://compound.mov/), supplied by the user, not another product named Compound. **Observed:** the installed `/Applications/Compound.app` package reports version **0.207.8**. After the user completed email verification, the app displayed its Projects dashboard, then an empty project with an editable timeline, preview, chat, file attachment and agent/model controls. No user footage was imported and no edit, export or posting operation was performed. Features below are confirmed from the installed vendor documentation, not demonstrated by a rendered test.

- **Confirmed:** native video elements support fitting/cropping, timing and audio gain; groups let the frame, footage and overlays move together. Keyframes animate position, scale, rotation, opacity and other properties with standard easing, custom Bezier curves and spring presets. This covers deliberate interface punch-ins and smooth callouts. [Video](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/video.md), [groups](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/group.md), [keyframes](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/keyframes.md).
- **Confirmed:** shadows, blur and masks are available, alongside text entrance/exit presets and standard transitions. A blur filter is not evidence of physically correct motion blur. [Styles](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/styles.md), [animations](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/animations.md), [transitions](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/transitions.md).
- **Confirmed:** HTML motion graphics can use Anime.js with playhead-driven timing. The vendor documents capture/export problems with certain nested opacity and CSS scale patterns; native composition elements are the straightforward choice for video zooms. HTML hosts reject video/audio/canvas tags, so dropping a video inside a CSS phone mockup is not a supported shortcut. [HTML reference](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/html.md).
- **Confirmed:** canvas surfaces support external Three.js/WebGL renderers, with an example 3D scene driven by the composition clock. This is a programmable route to custom 3D devices, not evidence of a ready-made phone template or turnkey video-texture synchronization. Packages must be installed in the project; buffers, asset loading and output density need explicit handling. **Inferred:** useful for a reusable custom device rig later; preset rendered clips are faster for this film. [Surface reference](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/surface-paint.md), [project modules](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/jsx/module.md).
- **Confirmed:** exports default to **1080p H.264 MP4/AAC**. The project can specify shorter-side resolution **2160**, fps and bitrate, plus supported containers/codecs. A landscape 16:9 scene at 2160 becomes 3840×2160; supported combinations depend on the machine. The `.mov` extension alone does not establish ProRes or alpha support. [Export reference](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/tools/export.md).
- **Confirmed:** frame capture renders the scene the export would encode, and structural checks can be run before export. The app exposes a local MCP server with project/media/render tools and documents registration with Codex and other agents. This session read the bundled docs; it did not configure a new MCP connection. [Capture](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/tools/capture.md), [tool catalog](/Applications/Compound.app/Contents/Resources/docs/reference/tools/README.md).
- **Confirmed:** Compound's website lists **$35/month after a 14-day trial requiring a card**, using the user's own Claude/Codex/Cursor plan. Existing access can avoid buying another editor, but it is not a permanently free tool. Account-specific entitlements were not checked. [Official product/pricing](https://compound.mov/).

## Editing pipeline for the existing ReSpace film

All recommendations in this section are **Inferred production judgements**, grounded in the existing [three-minute storyboard](respace-launch-script-storyboard.md); they do not revise the script or app specification.

1. **Prepare one coherent demo session.** Use the same Room, budget, questions, chosen Products and design versions across the capture. Record scanning on the supported physical iPhone. Use the native screen recorder or QuickTime: connect iPhone to Mac, choose File → New Movie Recording, then select the connected iPhone screen source in the capture menu. Save an undecorated master.
2. **Capture actions separately.** Record the scan, Kept choices/budget, brief, questions, design reveal, Product page, smaller-lamp edit and community/share preparation in short takes. Leave about two seconds of still UI at each end. Keep the original files; a 4K mockup export cannot restore detail absent from them.
3. **Assemble the narration and story in Compound first.** Establish the full three-minute order with rough footage, then trim while preserving enough time to read questions, the proposal and Product details. Introduce effects after this timing works.
4. **Use three visual treatments.** Real hand/phone footage connects the app to the dorm; near-flat screen close-ups explain decisions; two or three 3D-phone shots add emphasis. Keep phones near frontal while text must be read. Smooth acceleration/deceleration, consistent direction, soft shadow and a restrained background are the starting point.
5. **Render device shots separately.** In FreeMockup, start with a five-second recording and one free iPhone preset. Export with a baked background matching the film and import it into Compound. Verify the result before committing further shots. Full 3D camera changes then require re-rendering that source shot; Compound can trim and compose the resulting clip.
6. **Build focused zooms and graphics in Compound.** Use native video/group keyframes for punch-ins, text and shapes for highlights. For the existing script, use a restrained phone reveal around 00:24, enlarge the real generated question around 00:40–00:58, hold the first proposal around 00:58, and match the smaller-lamp request/result after 01:50. Preserve the storyboard's exact shot timing when making the edit; these ranges are navigation aids.
7. **Finish once.** Balance narration, licensed music and a few quiet action/movement sounds; match the filmed material's color while keeping app UI colors faithful. Use a consistent 16:9 delivery frame rate (30fps is a practical starting point if compatible with the camera footage). Draft at 1080p; explicitly select 2160 for the final UHD master if sources and the machine support it. Check actual output dimensions, readable prices/text, transition frames, sound and the final CTA. Export a separate vertical re-edit for social use if needed.

**Suggested Compound brief:** “Use the supplied ReSpace storyboard and footage to assemble a three-minute 16:9 launch film. Start with narration and story timing, then add the supplied 3D-phone clips at the product introduction and reveal. Use native editable zooms to direct attention to the generated question, Product price/dimensions and smaller-lamp result. Keep each interface readable before moving. Use consistent eased motion, understated shadows and restrained sound. Preserve the app pixels and displayed data. Capture representative frames to check layout and render a short test before the full final export.”

**Inferred zero-additional-tool-cost route:** existing Compound access + native Apple capture + FreeMockup presets. **Strictly free editor alternative:** replace Compound with Resolve Free. Recordly is optional; it duplicates work Compound's native keyframes can already do.

## Zoom plug-in caution

**Confirmed:** current [MrAlexTech MagicZoom](https://www.mralextech.com/magiczoom) lists a $49.99 purchase and seven-day trial; its new free version is marked “coming soon.” Older tutorials refer to a previous free edition. **Inferred:** no zoom plug-in is required for this workflow; start with Compound's native keyframes or Resolve's standard tools rather than designing the film around a trial.
