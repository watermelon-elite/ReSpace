## Pacing, visual system and sound

The film alternates interviews, workplace action, interface inserts and jokes. Interview returns act as small resets, so a new feature is attached to a person’s experience rather than added to an endless software tour. The overloaded message montage and clean title card are a larger reset. After that, the film can carry more explanation because its basic contrast has already been established.

Floating graphics make work visible around the people doing it. The before/after difference is partly the **organization of graphic information**, not simply a different color grade. A small team could borrow that relationship using a limited overlay system and stable shots; extensive perspective tracking is optional. ## Strongest ReSpace applications — proposals

1. **Show why the person’s current process fragments the room decision.** A small number of mismatched inputs—a saved image, a real room constraint, an object worth keeping—can appear around the same person. The problem must be understandable without filling the screen with dozens of unrelated app logos. ReSpace then needs to connect those inputs through operations the build can actually demonstrate.
2. **Use a clean reveal to change the organization of information.** After a short burst of conflicting possibilities, give the room scan and intent choice space to read. The transition should feel useful because it makes a decision easier to understand, not merely because a logo appears over different music.
3. **Keep the person present during proof.** Compose a real phone insert with the room and the user’s reaction, then let the actual proposed 3D layout occupy a clear shot. A small joke or human reaction can follow the successful operation. The proof itself should remain legible.

**Small-team feasibility:** a real room, one or two credible performers, controlled interview/observational shots, actual app inserts and restrained motion graphics are realistic. Multi-location cast coverage and elaborate tracked UI would increase scheduling and post-production work. The film’s self-referential agency story is specific to its origin; ReSpace needs its own reason for a founder or character to speak.

**What not to copy:** the number of features, the 140-second duration, invented usage testimonials, or the raw model’s proposed holographic furniture and family collaboration interface. A proposed layout is different from live AR. The broader filmmaking lesson is to connect people, a clear problem and inspectable product actions.
