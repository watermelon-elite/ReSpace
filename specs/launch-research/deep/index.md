# ReSpace: twenty full-film audiovisual analyses

**Production follow-up:** the revised [three-minute launch script and 43-shot storyboard](../respace-launch-script-storyboard.md) develops this research into a landscape dorm-room story, with generated-question and product explanations, an imagined furnished future and short supporting sections. The [critical review](../respace-script-revision-review.md) connects the revisions to these references.

**All twenty selected films received complete video-and-audio analysis at 6 fps, covering about 32 minutes of source footage (31:47 in the combined duration inventory).** Each linked report breaks down the opening, chronological scenes, changes in pace, visual language, sound, product introduction, proof and payoff, then separates observations from ReSpace adaptations. Focused rechecks and editorial corrections are included where the first analysis was unreliable.

**The recommendation: start with a recognizable relationship between a person and a room, then let ReSpace answer the need already on screen.** The strongest combination is Pinterest's spatial continuity, Wispr's matched problem and proof, Descript's demonstration inside the story, and IKEA's view of rooms through the activities they support. reMarkable supplies a useful change in sensory intensity; Sonos supplies the return to everyday life.

Start with the [original ReSpace creative direction and proposed 65-second film](respace-direction.md), then use the comparison below to choose individual references. The [120-candidate discovery ledger](../candidates.md) and [original shortlist](../shortlist.md) preserve the selection stage. Numbers below retain that shortlist order; they are not a ranking of measured creative effectiveness or organic reach.

## The strongest principles for this launch

### 1. A bigger idea needs a small, observable situation

The strongest material for spatial personality is a person trying to live in a room: keeping an inherited object, finding room for a hobby, working, resting or welcoming someone. IKEA, Sonos and Floyd make environments meaningful through their use. Pinterest makes differences in taste visible. These are more concrete openings than an unsupported lecture about what a room does to the brain.

**Proposed ReSpace premise: “You've changed. Has your room?”** One recognizable situation can carry that larger thought. Establish it quickly, then keep the same need, room and personal object present through the demonstration. This is an original creative recommendation, not a quoted reference line or a tested hook.

### 2. The product should answer a question the opening created

Wispr returns to earlier frustrations; Descript edits the awkward material its characters produced. Their strongest structural lesson is preparation: the audience already understands why the next operation matters. For ReSpace, choose a working app result first, identify the need it answers, and write the opening around that need.

A motivated transition can follow a wall edge into scan geometry, a person's gesture into an action, or a repeated object into the proposal. An introductory thought followed by an unrelated screen tour would lose the causal connection these films build.

### 3. Energy does not require constant cutting

Pinterest changes the room within stable geography. Playdate pauses to make an input and response readable. reMarkable changes sensory density at the reveal. Floyd alternates components with human use. Dropbox's many cuts are coherent because visual forms connect them; its [director-reported 141 shots](https://motionographer.com/2015/11/02/interview-with-johnny-kelly-dropbox-creative-freedom/) are a particular production choice, not a target for this film.

Use a quick opening, a readable product action, and a held result. The unfamiliar software operation should receive more attention than a familiar physical joke. No defensible universal cuts-per-second or retention formula emerged from these sources.

### 4. Sound should clarify the action and the turn

The references use different approaches: narration and character speech, music-led UI sequences, tactile objects, and music that becomes part of domestic life. ReSpace can begin with an identifiable room sound, carry its rhythm into the scan, and give the result a clear musical or sonic change. That design must follow the chosen scene.

One useful correction: IKEA's *Make Room for Life* keeps music running through the hush gesture into dancing. Its contrast is behavioral; the initial automated claim of a silence drop was wrong. A visually dramatic moment does not necessarily have a dramatic audio break.

### 5. Show the human consequence after the proof

Sonos and IKEA return attention to people inhabiting space. ReSpace's result should answer the opening activity, with the same room orientation and personal object making the change understandable. A staged physical after-scene must be distinguishable from the app's proposal. Finish with one action that matches availability: access, waitlist or download.

The [creative direction](respace-direction.md) turns these principles into a proposed 65-second sequence, three opening variants, production choices and a way to evaluate the first animatic.

## What the full viewing changed

- **Product-first references have a narrower job.** Notion Mail, Notion AI, Granola, Daylight and IKEA Place are useful for product presentation or proof; they do not all begin with the broad worldview suggested by their surrounding campaign copy.
- **First naming, first visibility and first demonstration differ.** Slack names the product before its formal title card. Headspace names the service before showing its interface. The individual reports distinguish these events instead of treating the end card as the reveal.
- **Metaphors need an explicit interpretation.** Pinterest, HomePod and IKEA Kreativ use physical transformations to express an idea. They do not establish that software instantly changes a real room.
- **Benefit, proof and claim are different.** Headspace's opening depicts desired benefits. Floyd's domestic scenes are not verified customer testimonials. Sonos shows several households. None should become unsupported causal claims in a ReSpace script.

## Method, coverage and how to use the files

The user-requested [watch-video skill](/Users/humaid/.codex/skills/watch-video/SKILL.md) received actual video and audio across each film's entire runtime, using explicit full-length intervals at 6 fps. Seven full passes used the default Gemini Flash model and thirteen used Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite after the default model reached its daily quota. Source metadata, complete-span contact sheets and supplemental captions or production sources were used where available to check conflicts. Captions did not replace audiovisual analysis.

This is automated sampled-frame/audio analysis with editorial review, not a claim of human real-time viewing of every encoded frame. Model timestamps are navigation aids, not frame-accurate edit instructions. The per-film coverage records state the exact requested intervals, model, source version and limitations. Additional focused passes checked Slack's opening, Dollar Shave Club's middle, IKEA's sound transition, Headspace's opening and HomePod's early product interaction; their findings are recorded with each film.

Each folder contains the editorial `report.md`, a `coverage.json`, and linked audiovisual output. Where an output reproduced excessive source wording or lyrics, the delivered version contains **explicit, minimal quotation redactions**; observations and timestamps are otherwise retained. Untouched originals remain in the private working files. The editorial report governs interpretation when the generated output contains a contradiction or unsupported claim.

Two provenance distinctions matter: Tylko was analyzed through the credited sound studio's complete Bedroom film, without asserting byte-for-byte identity to the originally selected embed; Headspace used the director-linked public YouTube version. HomePod used the complete selected Vimeo film, rather than relying on the earlier shorter YouTube preview source. Granola's original selected Vimeo asset was recovered and analyzed in full.

The [coverage summary](coverage-summary.json) provides a compact machine-readable inventory. This deep pass evaluates creative construction; it does not establish paid-versus-organic reach, retention curves, or which creative choice caused a view count. Proposed ReSpace scenes remain separate from capabilities verified in a running app.
