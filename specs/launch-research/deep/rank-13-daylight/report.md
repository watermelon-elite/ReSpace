## Strongest ReSpace borrowing — interpretation

Show what the existing room makes difficult, then demonstrate the same person's intended activity becoming easier to imagine in the proposed layout. For example, shoot a real path through the room, show its constraints in the captured model, and return to the corresponding part of the proposal. Human action supplies the criterion for judging the design.

Use material close-ups sparingly to give a technical 3D sequence physical relevance: a worn desk corner, a keepsake, a fabric texture, or light on the wall. The camera can return to these details after the design appears. This makes personal attachment visible without inventing scientific claims about personality.

Borrow the restraint for the moment when the audience must understand the result. Borrowing the entire contemplative tempo would conflict with the requested fast launch style. A comparative frame could be especially economical: same room geometry, same camera angle, different proposal, with one clear benefit highlighted.
