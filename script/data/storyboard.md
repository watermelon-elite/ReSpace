# ReSpace launch film script and storyboard

A barren dorm could belong to anyone. Its new owner needs somewhere to study, make room for friends and work out what feels like him. ReSpace turns those intentions into specific questions, a taste profile and a proposed room with real products. A brief café and office demonstration broadens the idea; a blooming flower returns us to the life this room could hold.

## Format and performance

- **Master:** 03:00, 16:9 landscape, 47 timed editorial beats. Several beats share continuous camera takes.
- **Spoken copy:** 262 words, approximately 87 words per minute across the film. Read connected phrases conversationally, leaving the marked pauses for readable interfaces, performance and sound. Technical and affiliate sections retain seven-second reservations without finished narration.
- **Product timing:** ReSpace enters at 00:24; the first complete proposal appears at 00:58. The generated questions and Product card keep their original readable holds.
- **Cast:** one student, two friends and a separate ReSpace team presenter, with an external opening narrator.
- **Production:** real people use the existing bed, desk and chair. Composite proposed furnishings around them; no furniture purchase is required. The café, office and flower are separate visual inserts.
- **Continuity:** repeat the doorway, notebook, card deck and friends’ photograph. Keep the saved room proposal beside the photograph in the final real-room scene.
- **CTA:** ReSpace. Make room for you. Join the waitlist.

### Performance roles and track grammar

**Opening narrator, 00:00–00:24:** an external dramatic storyteller observes the student. The student never speaks to the lens. At 00:23.5, let the team presenter’s breath and matching room tone arrive under the student’s final image; cut to the presenter for “Meet ReSpace.” Keep the musical phrase continuous.

**ReSpace team, 00:24 onward:** the presenter speaks from the left side of the same dorm, leaving space for demo panels on the right. Their voice continues over full-screen demos, the commercial-space examples, flower and acted evening. The opening narrator does not return. Read the flower line with a quieter, slower delivery, then carry that warmth into “Now picture a night here.”

**Base:** full-frame footage, the presenter, full-screen app captures, the flower macro or a neutral graphic background.

**Overlay:** independently timed story inserts, app panels, commercial-space designs, comparison insets and furnishings-only composites. Keep the real actors and contact surfaces in Base.

**Top overlay:** short labels and attention accents, clear of faces, controls and captions. Café · Connection and Office · Focus identify the commercial designs; Imagined future identifies the acted furnished dorm.

**Subtitles and sound:** caption all speech with selective emphasis. Sound and music stay in shot notes, without an audio lane. The screen anchors cards to words rather than elapsed time; silent shots share the preceding narration anchors while retaining their own timings.

## Production notes

### Commercial spaces and the flower

Keep the team presenter in the same dorm for the entire commercial passage. A café design enters beside them at 01:23.5 and gives way to an office design at 01:29.5. The office panel clears at 01:36 so the final thought lands on the presenter. Café · Connection changes to Office · Focus with the panels. Capture both designs from the completed app. Show conversation and concentration as design intentions, without promising measured social or productivity outcomes.

At 01:39, cut to one full-screen macro time-lapse of a real flower opening, warmly side-lit against a dark, softly blurred background. Hold the bloom for the whole line, “A flower blooms in the right environment.” Use a recorded or licensed time-lapse; this is a planned asset, not an existing shot. Avoid competing panels and extra headlines. Let a sustained musical note open with the petals; bring the notebook rustle in just before the next cut. Match the petal movement and warm palette to the student opening his notebook, then reveal the familiar desk.

The bloom is a visual metaphor for the film’s environment theme. The immediate return to the unfinished assignment gives it a specific human meaning.

### Technical reservation

Use the same dorm plan to show Measure / Plan / Check in seven seconds. Measured structure becomes a proposed arrangement, then a visible fit check. If technical labels help, associate RoomPlan/LiDAR and Perceptron with measurement and perception, Gemini with planning, and the solver with placement checks. Develop precise wording around the pipeline that is actually demonstrated; avoid a provider-logo wall.

Research-informed layout can be a restrained caption when a demonstrated rule uses that research. Show the concrete concern, such as desk access, rather than asserting a psychologically optimal room.

### Affiliate and freemium reservation

Follow the familiar lamp’s Product link to a retailer, with a branch showing affiliate revenue supporting a generous free tier. Label this Intended model. No earned revenue, signed merchant agreements, commission rates or conversion numbers are asserted. The seven-second slot remains brainstorming space, distinct from the earlier café and office use cases.

### Imagined evening without buying furniture

Use two locked camera positions: a doorway wide and a desk/bed medium. Capture clean plates and mark tripod, lens and actor positions. Keep exposure, focus and white balance fixed. The student studies at the real desk; the friends sit on the real bed; cards land on the existing bed. Keep every contact surface real.

Prepare furnishings-only overlays for the study angle, 01:44–01:52, and game angle, 01:52–02:06. Match the actual proposal’s lamp, storage and art to the camera perspective and lighting. Preserve real shadows and mask people passing in front of additions. Apply any crop to footage and furnishings together. Keep added objects out of the departing friend’s path. A small Imagined future label persists across both angles.

Film the game as a continuous performance: a small win, friend rises, another hand is dealt, friend notices, sits back down, laughter. Preserve the cause and reaction while shortening repeated setup. The same friends and cards already appeared in the opening. The app’s own design views contain no generated people.

Use actual generated questions; do not invent a taste-profile dashboard or imply every answer creates a new branch. Make processing edits visible.
